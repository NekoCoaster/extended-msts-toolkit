# NEMT 1.2.1

## What's new

- Opt-in **Enable Walking (Experimental)**, directly below **Unlock camera modes during derailment**. Press backtick to enter or leave first-person mode; customize the toggle and share the Route Editor's movement bindings.
- Walk at 3 m/s or toggle noclip with V to fly at 17 m/s. Hold left Shift for double speed or left Alt for half speed. Hold RMB to look with the cursor hidden. Space jumps to half the current eye height.
- In ground mode, Q/E adjusts eye height on a 0.05 m grid, from 0.1 to 100 m. Held input accelerates after a configurable delay (500 ms by default), using 60 Hz repeat ticks. This does not force a minimum rendering frame rate.
- Mouse wheel and -/= adjust FOV between 1 and 179 degrees on five-degree stops. Number-row 8 restores configured eye height and entry FOV without leaving FPV.
- Extended F5 shows walking/noclip state, world position, height, FOV, train-input blocking and controls.
- Train driving controls are blocked during FPV, while function keys and Escape remain available. Native camera-selection actions switch out of FPV when accepted, except 8, which resets FPV settings.
- Guarded camera transitions address the reported derailment/FPV crashes. **Unlock camera modes during derailment** now also preserves the current camera instead of forcing the derailment view.
- Leaving noclip above terrain preserves horizontal momentum while falling; leaving it below terrain snaps the feet to ground.

## Installation and controls

Close MSTS, open the updated NEMT, select **Enable Walking (Experimental)** and
click **Apply**, then restart MSTS. Restarting alone does not deploy the update.
The original F12 toggle setting migrates to backtick. See the
[walking guide](../docs/walking.md) for all controls, INI settings and limits.

## Compatibility and validation

Walking is experimental and uses terrain-only grounding: there is no scenery,
platform, bridge or train collision capsule. Secure the train first; its
simulation continues while driving input is blocked. Extreme FOV values cause
strong perspective distortion. Arbitrary routes, tile boundaries, physical
joysticks and multiplayer still need broader validation.

Local validation passed the native regression suite (including 508 walking-model
and 328 GUI checks), camera/input adapter tests, readiness harness, 18 feature
configurations, source guards, repository-tool tests and XP-baseline import
audits. User-reported walking, zoom and camera-fix success is separate from
automated evidence; no new cross-platform or universal compatibility claim is
made. See [development evidence](../docs/technical/walking-development.md).
