# NEMT 1.2.3

## FPV flashlight

- L toggles a camera-following flashlight in walking and noclip. It switches off
  when leaving FPV; range, angle and brightness tuning persists for the session.
- The FPV beam illuminates terrain, supported scenery/train materials and the
  alternate track shaders alongside the native locomotive headlight.
- [ / ] narrow/widen the beam; , / . shorten/extend range; ; / ' decrease/increase
  brightness. Each step is 5 degrees, 5 m or 5 percentage points.
- Holding an adjustment key repeats after HeightRepeatDelayMs (default 500 ms),
  then every 100 ms. L remains one toggle per press.
- Shift-Z can toggle the native compass/FPS display in FPV. Train controls remain
  isolated; custom movement bindings retain priority over flashlight tuning.
- Expanded the bounded native hook registry to 1024 entries, preventing the
  walking startup failure when the combined feature hooks exceeded 64 entries.

## Settings

Under [Walking] in NEMT/settings.ini:

```ini
FlashlightRangeM=45
FlashlightAngleDeg=70
FlashlightBrightnessPct=100
HeightRepeatDelayMs=500
```

Range accepts 5–1000 m; full beam angle 10–150 degrees; brightness 0–100 percent.
Defaults remain 45 m, 70 degrees and 100 percent. Invalid values fall back per
setting; Apply preserves valid custom values. HeightRepeatDelayMs accepts
50–5000 ms and also controls the initial delay for height adjustment.
Live adjustments do not rewrite the INI. Extended F5 shows current light settings.

## Updating and validation

Close MSTS, open the updated NEMT, click **Apply**, then restart MSTS.

Midnight Marias Pass live probes showed simultaneous locomotive/FPV lighting,
including rails, sleepers and ballast, and L off/on comparisons. The user
confirmed the scene lights as expected. Native regression tests cover settings,
repeat/cancellation, display-action isolation, light lifecycle, colour handling
and hook-capacity bounds. The final 1000 m/repeat/Shift-Z additions await a
fresh-launch live check.

This remains experimental vertex lighting: sparse meshes can produce coarse
beam edges, large ranges increase terrain refresh work, and not every route or
material is certified. No shadows or AI-headlight sources are added. An earlier
instrumented-probe crash remains historical unresolved evidence; later bounded
checks are not a long-term stability guarantee. See the [walking guide](../docs/walking.md)
and [technical record](../docs/technical/walking.md).
